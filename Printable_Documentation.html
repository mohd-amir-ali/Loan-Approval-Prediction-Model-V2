Automated Loan Approval Prediction & Explainable Credit Risk Underwriting via Pruned CART Decision Trees

Author: Mohd Amir Ali (Roll No: 160920733019) · B.E. Computer Science & Engineering, Lords Institute of Engineering & Technology (Osmania University, Hyderabad) · Portfolio: techwithamir.com · Certificate ID: 23DST1DSI22

1. Executive Overview & Problem Statement

Retail banks and housing finance institutions evaluate thousands of loan applications daily. Manual underwriting is slow, inconsistent, and vulnerable to subjective human bias, whereas black-box deep learning models violate banking transparency mandates (Basel II/III Internal Ratings-Based guidelines and Equal Credit Opportunity adverse-action disclosure laws).

This project implements an explainable, automated credit risk underwriting platform powered by a Best-First Pruned CART Decision Tree Classifier (DecisionTreeClassifier(max_leaf_nodes=25, criterion='gini')) trained on 614 historical loan applications (LoanApprovalPrediction.csv). The model achieves 86.56% Training Accuracy and 82.80% Testing Accuracy (85:15 split, random_state=5) while providing an exact root-to-leaf boolean decision trace, monthly EMI and Fixed Obligation to Income Ratio (FOIR) stress testing, and counterfactual recommendations.

2. Core Machine Learning Algorithm & Mathematical Formulation

2.1 Why CART Decision Tree (DecisionTreeClassifier) for Credit Risk?

  • White-Box Regulatory Auditability: Every approval or rejection is traced through explicit boolean rules (e.g., Credit_History ≤ 0.5 → LoanAmount ≤ 310k → FOIR ≤ 55%), satisfying mandatory borrower adverse-action disclosures.
  • Non-Linear Conditional Interactions: A high loan amount is risky for a low single income, yet safe for a dual-income household with a clean bureau record. Hierarchical tree splits capture these conditional interactions natively.
  • Invariance to Income Skewness: Retail income distributions are heavily right-skewed; CART threshold splits depend only on rank order and do not suffer from parametric distortion.

2.2 Gini Impurity & Information Gain Equations

1. Node Gini Impurity: G(t) = 1 − ∑ [ p(c | t) ]² = 1 − (p₁² + p₀²)
   • Root Node (N₀ = 614: 422 Approved [68.73%], 192 Rejected [31.27%]):
     G(Root) = 1 − (0.6873² + 0.3127²) = 1 − (0.4724 + 0.0978) = 0.4298

2. Gini Impurity Reduction (Information Gain ΔG):
   ΔG(t, f, θ) = G(t) − [ (N_L / N_t)·G(t_L) + (N_R / N_t)·G(t_R) ]
   • Root Split on Credit_History ≤ 0.5:
     Left Child (CH=0, N_L=89: 7 Approved, 82 Rejected) ➔ G(t_L) = 0.1449
     Right Child (CH=1, N_R=525: 415 Approved, 110 Rejected) ➔ G(t_R) = 0.3312
     ΔG = 0.4298 − [ (89/614)(0.1449) + (525/614)(0.3312) ] = 0.4298 − 0.3042 = +0.1256

2.3 Best-First Leaf-Node Pruning (max_leaf_nodes=25)

To prevent overfitting on noisy individual borrower records, the tree growth is constrained to max_leaf_nodes=25 (and cross-checked with max_depth=5, min_samples_leaf=5). Using a priority queue ordered by weighted impurity reduction Nt · ΔG(t), only the 24 most statistically significant credit splits are retained.

3. Fair-Lending Feature Engineering & Preprocessing Pipeline

From the 13 raw columns in LoanApprovalPrediction.csv, 3 columns are deliberately excluded prior to training:

  • Loan_ID: Alphanumeric primary key (LP001002–LP002990) with zero predictive generalization.
  • Gender & Married: Excluded (`df.drop(["Gender", "Married"], axis=1)`) to enforce Fair-Lending & Equal Credit Opportunity compliance. Empirical correlation analysis confirms near-zero linear relationship with loan approval (r = 0.02 and r = 0.09), while household earning strength is captured objectively via CoapplicantIncome.
